🚐 Airport transfers, both ways

Recommended: three 9-seat private vans, door to door. Fourteen people plus twenty-eight checked bags will not fit in one or two vans, so three is the realistic number. Drivers track the flight, wait up to 90 minutes after landing, meet the group in the arrivals hall with a name sign, and drop everyone at the hotel door. That is the no-hassle option without the price of a full coach: roughly ¥6,200 (₱2,340) per person each way, against ¥17,700 (₱6,700) for a single private coach. Across the group that saves about ₱17,400 per couple.

Why not the train or airport bus? The arrival lands at 20:00 and the last Narita Express leaves at 21:44, which is possible but leaves no margin if the flight is delayed, plus a taxi hop from the station with all the bags. The departure needs a 05:45 pickup, and no train or airport bus runs that early. A private vehicle is the only option that covers both days properly.

❄️ Hakone private guided day · Jan 21

A private guided day: Owakudani volcanic valley, the Lake Ashi cruise under Mount Fuji, Hakone Shrine, and the clearest Fuji views of the year. About 1 hour 45 minutes each way, hotel pickup and drop-off.

How to book. GetYourGuide and Klook both list private chartered Hakone day tours with an English-speaking driver, and the big advantage is buyer protection: free cancellation up to 24 hours before, and on GetYourGuide you can reserve now and pay later. The catch is that platform charter vehicles usually cap around 10 seats, so fourteen people means booking two vehicles. Alternatively CAB STATION can quote a single micro bus for all fourteen, which keeps the group in one vehicle but is paid upfront.

Whichever is chosen: confirm the total headcount of fourteen in writing, insist on winter tyres since Hakone's mountain roads frost in January, pay by card, and get the vehicle, guide, date, price and cancellation terms confirmed before any deposit.

🎌 Two optional extras in Kabukicho

Samurai Restaurant is the loud, neon, samurai-and-ninja dinner show in Kabukicho, successor to the old Robot Restaurant. Gloriously silly, about two hours, and a good pre-drinks activity for anyone who likes a spectacle. A short walk from the hotel.

Thermae-Yu onsen, entirely optional and not part of the plan. It is a real natural hot spring in the middle of Kabukicho, open 24 hours, around ¥2,200 (₱830) booked online. Worth knowing how it works: Japanese onsen are traditionally bathed without swimwear, in separate male and female areas. That is the norm in Japan, but it is understandably not for everyone, so this is listed purely as a personal option rather than a group activity. The tattoo policy is also strict, so anyone with tattoos should check the rules before going.

Thursday · January 21

Hakone: Fuji, Lake and Onsen

Your first proper day out, and the counterweight to five nights of Shinjuku. A private guided day trip into the mountains: a volcanic valley, a lake cruise under Mount Fuji, and the clearest Fuji views of the whole year. All fourteen together with an English-speaking guide, the same setup as the Nikko day.

The Hakone Day

Thursday

1

🚌 Private pickup from the hotel

⏰ ~8:00 AM · Hakone is about 90 km southwest · 1 hr 45 min drive

The guide and vehicle collect the group from the hotel door. Hakone sits roughly 90 to 100 km from Tokyo, about one hour forty-five each way, so it is a comfortable day trip on the same rhythm as Nikko: out mid-morning, a full day in the mountains, back in Shinjuku for dinner.

2

🌋 Owakudani volcanic valley and the black eggs

⏰ Late morning · Ropeway over the steaming valley

Ride the Hakone Ropeway up over Owakudani, a live volcanic valley where sulphur steam drifts from the hillside and the whole place smells of the earth's engine room. The tradition here is the kuro-tamago, eggs boiled black in the hot springs, said to add seven years to your life. On a clear winter morning the views back across to Fuji from the ropeway are extraordinary.

📍 Owakudani📍 Hakone Ropeway
3

⛵ The Lake Ashi pirate ship, with Fuji on the water

⏰ Early afternoon · ~30 to 40 min cruise across the lake

Down to Lake Ashi, a caldera lake formed by an eruption 3,000 years ago, and cross it on the retro galleon-style cruise boat. This is the postcard: the red Heiwa no Torii gate of Hakone Shrine rising straight out of the water, with Mount Fuji on the horizon behind it. Winter brings the clearest air of the year, so on a good day Fuji reflects in the lake surface. Stand out on the open deck for the photos even though it is cold, it is worth it.

📍 Lake Ashi📍 Hakone Shrine torii
4

⛩️ Hakone Shrine and a warm-up before the drive

⏰ Mid-afternoon · Then the road back to Shinjuku

Finish at Hakone Shrine, founded in 757, its vermilion gate and cedar-lined steps running down to the lake. Then the drive home, back in Shinjuku with time to warm up before dinner.

Friday · January 22 · ❄️ The Snow Day

Nikko in the Snow

Is Nikko far or hard? No. Two to two and a half hours north in a warm private vehicle that handles the icy mountain road. The one day worth an early alarm.

Guided Day Two

Friday
Early pickup → UNESCO shrine → Frozen waterfall → Snow lake → Hot-pot dinner
1

🚌 Private pickup at the hotel

⏰ ~7:30 AM lobby pickup · The one early morning, worth it

A proper winter-tired vehicle and guide collect the group from the hotel and drive north. All you do is look out the window as Tokyo gives way to snow country. Grab a convenience-store coffee and onigiri for the road.

2

⛩️ Toshogu Shrine and Shinkyo Bridge

⏰ Late morning · A UNESCO World Heritage site in the cedars

The gilded, gloriously over-the-top Toshogu Shrine, resting place of the shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu, wearing snow. The famous sleeping cat, the three wise monkeys, gold leaf against dark cedar. On the way in, the vermilion arc of the Shinkyo Bridge over the icy river. There are stairs and uneven stone, so mind the footing, but this is a walking group and it is glorious.

📍 Toshogu Shrine 📍 Shinkyo Bridge
3

❄️ Kegon Falls, turned to ice

⏰ Afternoon · Up the Irohazaka switchbacks · Elevator ~9:00 AM to 4:30 PM in winter

Up the winding Irohazaka road to Kegon Falls, a near 100-metre cascade that in deep winter partly freezes into pale blue ice, one of Nikko's great sights and the whole reason to come in January. Then the still, white expanse of Lake Chuzenji and the sweeping view from Akechidaira.

📍 Kegon Falls 📍 Lake Chuzenji
Booking and winter notes: all the operators and links for this day are in the Booking Desk at the top of this page. Whichever is chosen, state the group size of fourteen and confirm winter tyres in writing. Dress properly warm, this is mountain cold, well below Tokyo, with snow underfoot. The Kegon elevator runs shorter winter hours (about 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM), and a private guide simply reroutes if snow closes anything higher up. Reserve by November.
🍲 Dinner: something to thaw you out

Back in Tokyo around 7:00 PM after a day in the snow, so tonight is hot pot at MO-MO-PARADISE Kabukicho, the original branch, open since 1993 and under 10 minutes on foot from the hotel: all-you-can-eat shabu-shabu and sukiyaki with F1 wagyu, free-flow vegetables wheeled round on carts, and an all-you-can-drink add-on. Unlimited meat and unlimited beer after a snow day, with nobody counting.

Where You Are Actually Eating

Not vague suggestions. These are named places, each one checked as currently open and each one with a real reputation behind it: long-standing institutions, or spots that people consistently rate and recommend. Every one of them can handle fourteen, which rules out most of Tokyo's famous small restaurants.

Thu Jan 21 · Welcome dinner
🍶 Banya Shinjuku Higashiguchi · Sanchome

Your welcome feast. Chosen for one reason above all: it genuinely fits fourteen in one private room with a door, which almost nothing characterful in Tokyo does. Private rooms take 2 to 60 guests, the courses come with all-you-can-drink, and it takes instant online reservations with no sign-up, so you can lock it tonight from Bicol. Their signature is the Oni-mori, an absurd seven-fish sashimi platter, plus yakitori and hot pots. Open Sundays, about 10 minutes on foot from the hotel.

🥩 MO-MO-PARADISE · Kabukicho

The original branch, opened in Kabukicho in 1993, under 10 minutes on foot from the hotel. All-you-can-eat shabu-shabu and sukiyaki with F1 wagyu, free-flow vegetables wheeled round on carts, and an all-you-can-drink add-on. This is the right meal after a freezing day in Nikko: everyone round a bubbling pot, unlimited meat, unlimited beer, nobody counting.

Wed 20 & Sat 23 · The alleys
🏮 Omoide Yokocho, then Golden Gai

Best done in this order, because they do different jobs. Omoide Yokocho is the food alley: around 80 tiny stalls wedged against the train tracks, open-fronted charcoal grills, yakitori at roughly ¥150 to 250 a skewer, beer ¥600 to 800, highballs ¥400 to 600. No reservations, no cover charge at most stalls, turnover is fast so if the stools are full just try the next one. Best between 7 and 10 PM. The one people always name is Kabuto, a no-frills stall that grills only freshwater eel, and Bar Albatross for its chandelier.

Golden Gai is the second stop, after dinner, and it is about drinking rather than eating: over 200 miniature themed bars, each seating 5 to 10, which is exactly why fourteen of you will split into clusters. For bars that reliably welcome foreigners and have English-speaking staff, the names that come up again and again are Albatross G, Bar Plastic Model and Cambiare.

Two things worth knowing in Kabukicho. First, street touts in the area are known for overcharging scams, with Tokyo police logging more than 360 complaints in 2025. The rule is simple: do not follow anyone who approaches you on the street offering a bar or club, however friendly, and stick to the places listed here. Second, in Golden Gai, photographing the alleys needs permission from the local business association and photographing inside a bar needs the owner's consent, so always ask "Shashin ii desu ka?" first.
🗝️ The magic words when you book

Ask for a private room (koshitsu) and a course with nomihoudai (all-you-can-drink for a set time, usually 90 minutes to 2 hours). This fixes the price per head, keeps all fourteen in one room, and means nobody has to work out a split at the end of the night.

🛎️ Let the front desk do the calling

The hotel can phone and reserve tables for fourteen far more smoothly than you can from Bicol, especially anywhere without English online booking. Hand them your dinner list at check-in on Jan 20, or email ahead. For a big group in Tokyo this is the single best trick there is.

Shopping · What and Where

Everything Central and Walkable

Shinjuku is one of Tokyo's great shopping hubs, with Harajuku and Shibuya a few minutes away and Asakusa for souvenirs on the last day. Bring passports for tax-free over ¥5,000 per store, and remember you each have 2 checked bags, so shop without fear.

🏬 Shinjuku · Right at the Hotel

All within about 10 minutes of the hotel: Isetan, Tokyo's most famous department store, with a food hall worth the trip alone, plus NEWoMan and Lumine over the station, the enormous Bic Camera and Yodobashi electronics stores, and Uniqlo and drugstore flagships. Perfect for shopping right up until dinner.

🎁 Nakamise + Solamachi · Souvenir Central

On the Jan 24 Asakusa day, Nakamise Street is a wall of traditional gifts: senbei crackers, fans, yukata, sweets, keychains. Then Solamachi under the Skytree bundles Ghibli, Sanrio, Pokemon and local snacks in one warm mall. This is your pasalubong sweep in one afternoon.

💊 Drugstores · Tax-Free and Cheaper than Manila

Matsumoto Kiyoshi and other big drugstores are all over Shinjuku: skincare, sunscreen, Lululun masks, painkillers, vitamins, snacks. Spend over ¥5,000 in one store, show passports, pay tax-free. Best raid time: the Saturday shopping afternoon.

🧥 What to Wear · Real Winter, Colder in Nikko

The formula, every day: ① Heattech or thermal top and bottom → ② sweater or fleece → ③ an insulated winter jacket (padded or down, hooded) → ④ comfortable waterproof shoes with grip, warm socks, gloves, beanie, scarf. Tokyo sits around 3 to 10 degrees, usually crisp, dry and sunny. Nikko is far colder, often below freezing with snow, so add an extra layer for the Jan 22 mountain day. Buy kairo heat packs at the first convenience store and keep one in each pocket. Trains and malls are warm, so layers you can peel are the whole trick.

👥 Moving as a Group of Fourteen

Two simple habits keep a group of fourteen running smoothly: a group chat for anyone who wanders off, and a fixed meeting time and spot at every regroup. On the free days, splitting into smaller clusters is the whole point, as long as the booked lunch and dinner are the fixed points everyone returns to. The guided days need no such planning, since the guide keeps everyone together.

💴 Cash · Still Matters

Cards work in the big shops, but small izakaya, market snacks and especially Golden Gai bars (cash-only, small seating charge) are cash-first. Everyone should carry ¥15,000 to 20,000 (≈₱5,650 to 7,540). 7-Eleven ATMs reliably take foreign cards.

🚆 Getting Around · IC Cards

Your base is on top of Shinjuku Station, the world's busiest hub, so almost everywhere is a direct or one-change ride and the west-side spots are minutes away. Everyone gets a Suica or Welcome Suica IC card, or the mobile version on iPhone, for tap-and-go on every train, bus and convenience store. Note: Android phones bought abroad cannot load mobile Suica, so get the physical Welcome Suica.

🧳 Luggage · Fully Handled

Your PAL tickets include 2 checked pieces each (23 kg per piece), so big suitcases plus hand-carry are completely fine. The private vans carry everyone's bags door to door on both the arrival and departure days, so there is no train-and-suitcase juggling anywhere in this plan. On Jan 25, the hotel can hold nothing since you check out and leave straight for the airport, so pack the night before.
