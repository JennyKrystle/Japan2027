🎌 Two optional extras in Kabukicho

Samurai Restaurant is the loud, neon, samurai-and-ninja dinner show in Kabukicho, successor to the old Robot Restaurant. Gloriously silly, about two hours, and a good pre-drinks activity for anyone who likes a spectacle. A short walk from the hotel.

Thermae-Yu onsen, entirely optional and not part of the plan. It is a real natural hot spring in the middle of Kabukicho, open 24 hours, around ¥2,200 (₱830) booked online. Worth knowing how it works: Japanese onsen are traditionally bathed without swimwear, in separate male and female areas. That is the norm in Japan, but it is understandably not for everyone, so this is listed purely as a personal option rather than a group activity. The tattoo policy is also strict, so anyone with tattoos should check the rules before going.

You land at nearly 11 PM the night before, so this day starts late on purpose. No pickup time, no early alarm, nothing more than fifteen minutes from the hotel. A free self-guided walk around the neighbourhood, a free view over the whole city at sunset, and a welcome dinner 200 metres up.

🍜 An easy lunch, all together

3

🚶 A self-guided Shinjuku walk, all of it free

⏰ ~1:30 to 4:15 PM · All within a few minutes of the hotel · No guide, no booking, no tickets

Chill, together, and it costs nothing. Four stops, all walkable from the door, in an order that flows from quiet to neon to food. Wander at your own pace, stop for coffee whenever, and nobody is chasing a schedule.

① Hanazono Shrine · free, open all hours, five minutes away. A 17th-century Inari shrine tucked behind Kabukicho, with a path of vermilion torii gates. Businesspeople come here to pray for success. Going straight from neon signs to a 400-year-old shrine in five minutes is one of the things Tokyo does best.
② The Godzilla head and Don Quijote · free. The Godzilla head on top of Hotel Gracery does a short show on the hour from noon to 8 PM, and you see it perfectly well from street level. The best viewpoint is Shinjuku Central Road, right beside Don Quijote, which is worth wandering into for snacks, souvenirs and general chaos.
③ The 3D cat billboard · free. On the Cross Shinjuku Vision building at the station's east exit, and best seen from across the road. It was Tokyo's first 3D billboard and the cat does something different every few minutes. A guaranteed group photo, and it looks even better when you pass it again after dark.
④ Isetan depachika · free to browse. The basement food hall of Tokyo's most famous department store, and for a group that loves eating it is a small miracle: perfect fruit, wagyu, sweets laid out like jewellery, and plenty of samples. Buy something for the room or just look.

Then it is one Oedo line stop to Tochomae for the sunset view. If anyone would rather sit somewhere warm for a while instead, Kinokuniya's seven-floor bookshop nearby is a genuinely quiet reset.

4

🌇 Sunset over Tokyo, for free

⏰ ~4:45 PM · 45th floor, 202 m · Free admission · Sunset is around 5:00 PM

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observation deck is the best-value view in the city and it costs nothing. From 202 metres up on the 45th floor you get a 360-degree panorama: the Shinjuku skyscrapers below, Tokyo Skytree in the distance, and Mount Fuji on the horizon on a clear winter day, which late January often delivers. Arrive before sunset and stay as the lights come on. There is a cafe up there if anyone wants a drink, and a projection-mapping light show on the building from sunset onward.

Getting there: the Toei Oedo line runs direct from Higashi-Shinjuku, four minutes from the hotel, to Tochomae in about six minutes, and Tochomae connects straight into the building. Total door to door is roughly fifteen minutes. South deck 9:30 AM to 10:00 PM, North deck 9:30 AM to 5:30 PM, both open on Thursdays. There is a bag inspection at the entrance, so travel light and allow a few extra minutes.

Friday · January 22 · ❄️ The Snow Day

Nikko in the Snow

Is Nikko far or hard? No. Two to two and a half hours north in a warm private vehicle that handles the icy mountain road. The one day worth an early alarm.

Guided Day Two

Friday

1

🚌 Private pickup at the hotel

⏰ ~7:30 AM lobby pickup · The one early morning, worth it

A proper winter-tired vehicle and guide collect the group from the hotel and drive north. All you do is look out the window as Tokyo gives way to snow country. Grab a convenience-store coffee and onigiri for the road.

2

⛩️ Toshogu Shrine and Shinkyo Bridge

⏰ Late morning · A UNESCO World Heritage site in the cedars

The gilded, gloriously over-the-top Toshogu Shrine, resting place of the shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu, wearing snow. The famous sleeping cat, the three wise monkeys, gold leaf against dark cedar. On the way in, the vermilion arc of the Shinkyo Bridge over the icy river. There are stairs and uneven stone, so mind the footing, but this is a walking group and it is glorious.

📍 Toshogu Shrine 📍 Shinkyo Bridge
3

🥢 Lunch at Nikko Maruhide Shokudo

⏰ ~12:30 PM · Walking distance from Toshogu · Car park nearby for the coach

The sensible lunch stop for fourteen. Over eighty years old, it handles tour groups at lunchtime as a matter of routine, and there is a dedicated car park a short walk away, which matters when you arrive by coach. The menu is built around yuba, Nikko's famous delicate tofu skin: the yubakatsudon and the tomato ramen with yuba are what people order. Generous portions, warm welcome, and vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free options, which is useful across fourteen people. Roughly ¥1,200 to 2,000 (₱450 to 750) each.

One place to avoid with this group: Tonkatsu Asai near Lake Chuzenji is excellent but has only six counter seats, and reviewers confirm that even groups get split up and queue outside. It cannot seat fourteen.
📍 Maruhide Shokudo📖 Tabelog
4

❄️ Kegon Falls, turned to ice

⏰ Afternoon · Up the Irohazaka switchbacks · Elevator ~9:00 AM to 4:30 PM in winter

Up the winding Irohazaka road to Kegon Falls, a near 100-metre cascade that in deep winter partly freezes into pale blue ice, one of Nikko's great sights and the whole reason to come in January. Then the still, white expanse of Lake Chuzenji and the sweeping view from Akechidaira.

📍 Kegon Falls 📍 Lake Chuzenji
Winter notes: the operator has confirmed winter tyres, which is the critical safety item on the Irohazaka switchbacks. Dress properly warm, this is mountain cold, well below Tokyo, with snow underfoot. The Kegon elevator runs shorter winter hours (about 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM), and a private guide simply reroutes if snow closes anything higher up. Reserve by November.
🍲 Dinner: something to thaw you out

Back in Tokyo around 7:00 PM after a day in the snow, so tonight is hot pot at MO-MO-PARADISE Kabukicho, the original branch, open since 1993 and under 10 minutes on foot from the hotel: all-you-can-eat shabu-shabu and sukiyaki with F1 wagyu, free-flow vegetables wheeled round on carts, and an all-you-can-drink add-on. Unlimited meat and unlimited beer after a snow day, with nobody counting.

Wed 20 & Sat 23 · The alleys
🏮 Omoide Yokocho, then Golden Gai

Best done in this order, because they do different jobs. Omoide Yokocho is the food alley: around 80 tiny stalls wedged against the train tracks, open-fronted charcoal grills, yakitori at roughly ¥150 to 250 a skewer, beer ¥600 to 800, highballs ¥400 to 600. No reservations, no cover charge at most stalls, turnover is fast so if the stools are full just try the next one. Best between 7 and 10 PM. The one people always name is Kabuto, a no-frills stall that grills only freshwater eel, and Bar Albatross for its chandelier.

Golden Gai is the second stop, after dinner, and it is about drinking rather than eating: over 200 miniature themed bars, each seating 5 to 10, which is exactly why fourteen of you will split into clusters. For bars that reliably welcome foreigners and have English-speaking staff, the names that come up again and again are Albatross G, Bar Plastic Model and Cambiare.

Two things worth knowing in Kabukicho. First, street touts in the area are known for overcharging scams, with Tokyo police logging more than 360 complaints in 2025. 🗝️ The magic words when you book

Ask for a private room (koshitsu) and a course with nomihoudai (all-you-can-drink for a set time, usually 90 minutes to 2 hours). This fixes the price per head, keeps all fourteen in one room, and means nobody has to work out a split at the end of the night.

🧥 What to Wear · Real Winter, Colder in Nikko

The formula, every day: ① Heattech or thermal top and bottom → ② sweater or fleece → ③ an insulated winter jacket (padded or down, hooded) → ④ comfortable waterproof shoes with grip, warm socks, gloves, beanie, scarf. Tokyo sits around 3 to 10 degrees, usually crisp, dry and sunny. Nikko is far colder, often below freezing with snow, so add an extra layer for the Jan 22 mountain day. Buy kairo heat packs at the first convenience store and keep one in each pocket. Trains and malls are warm, so layers you can peel are the whole trick.

👥 Moving as a Group of Fourteen

Two simple habits keep a group of fourteen running smoothly: a group chat for anyone who wanders off, and a fixed meeting time and spot at every regroup. On the free days, splitting into smaller clusters is the whole point, as long as the booked lunch and dinner are the fixed points everyone returns to. The guided days need no such planning, since the guide keeps everyone together.

💴 Cash · Still Matters

Cards work in the big shops, but small izakaya, market snacks and especially Golden Gai bars (cash-only, small seating charge) are cash-first. Everyone should carry ¥15,000 to 20,000 (≈₱5,650 to 7,540). 7-Eleven ATMs reliably take foreign cards.

🚆 Getting Around · IC Cards

Your base is on top of Shinjuku Station, the world's busiest hub, so almost everywhere is a direct or one-change ride and the west-side spots are minutes away. Everyone gets a Suica or Welcome Suica IC card, or the mobile version on iPhone, for tap-and-go on every train, bus and convenience store. Note: Android phones bought abroad cannot load mobile Suica, so get the physical Welcome Suica.

🧳 Luggage · Fully Handled

Your PAL tickets include 2 checked pieces each (23 kg per piece), so big suitcases plus hand-carry are completely fine. Tokyo Concierge carries everyone's bags door to door on the arrival and departure days and drives the group to Nikko, so there is no train-and-suitcase juggling anywhere in this plan. On Jan 25, the hotel can hold nothing since you check out and leave straight for the airport, so pack the night before.
