♨️ Two more Kabukicho things, both on your doorstep

Thermae-Yu is a genuine natural hot spring in the middle of Kabukicho, about 5 minutes from your hotel, fed by water piped from Naka-Izu. Six indoor baths, an open-air bath and saunas, open 24 hours. It is the perfect landing spot on the Jan 22 evening when you come back frozen from Nikko. Entry is around ¥2,700 to 2,800, or roughly ¥2,200 if you buy online in advance. One warning: the tattoo policy is strict and complicated, so anyone with ink should check before going rather than being turned away at the door.

The Samurai Restaurant is the loud, neon, samurai-and-ninja dinner show in Kabukicho, successor to the old Robot Restaurant. It is gloriously silly and about two hours long, which makes it a good pre-drinks activity for a group that likes a spectacle. Tickets are widely available and it is a few minutes from the hotel.

Thursday · January 21

Hakone: Fuji, Lake and Onsen

Your first proper day out, and the counterweight to five nights of Shinjuku. A private guided day trip into the mountains: a volcanic valley, a lake cruise under Mount Fuji, and the clearest Fuji views of the whole year. All fourteen together, one microbus, one guide, same as your Nikko day.

The Hakone Day

Thursday
~8 AM pickup → Owakudani + black eggs → Lake Ashi cruise → Hakone Shrine → back for dinner
1

🚌 Private pickup from the hotel

⏰ ~8:00 AM · Hakone is about 90 km southwest · 1 hr 45 min drive

Your guide and microbus collect all fourteen of you from the hotel door. Hakone sits roughly 90 to 100 km from Tokyo, about one hour forty-five each way by private vehicle, so it is a comfortable day trip on almost exactly the same rhythm as Nikko: out mid-morning, full day in the mountains, back in Shinjuku for dinner. Doze on the way, the driver handles everything.

2

🌋 Owakudani volcanic valley and the black eggs

⏰ Late morning · Ropeway over the steaming valley

Ride the Hakone Ropeway up over Owakudani, a live volcanic valley where sulphur steam drifts from the hillside and the whole place smells of the earth's engine room. The tradition here is the kuro-tamago, eggs boiled black in the hot springs, said to add seven years to your life. On a clear winter morning the views back across to Fuji from the ropeway are extraordinary.

📍 Owakudani📍 Hakone Ropeway
3

⛵ The Lake Ashi pirate ship, with Fuji on the water

⏰ Early afternoon · ~30 to 40 min cruise across the lake

Down to Lake Ashi, a caldera lake formed by an eruption 3,000 years ago, and cross it on the retro galleon-style cruise boat. This is the postcard: the red Heiwa no Torii gate of Hakone Shrine rising straight out of the water, with Mount Fuji on the horizon behind it. Winter brings the clearest air of the year, so on a good day Fuji reflects in the lake surface. Stand out on the open deck for the photos even though it is cold, it is worth it.

📍 Lake Ashi📍 Hakone Shrine torii
4

⛩️ Hakone Shrine and a warm-up before the drive

⏰ Mid-afternoon · Then the road back to Shinjuku

Finish at Hakone Shrine, founded in 757, its vermilion gate and cedar-lined steps running down to the lake. If the group would rather trade the shrine for a soak, tell the guide: Hakone is onsen country and there are day-use baths near the lake. Then the drive home, back in Shinjuku with time to warm up before dinner.

🚌 Private pickup at the hotel

A proper winter-tired vehicle and guide collect the group from the hotel and drive north. All you do is look out the window as Tokyo gives way to snow country. Grab a convenience-store coffee and onigiri for the road.

2

⛩️ Toshogu Shrine and Shinkyo Bridge

⏰ Late morning · A UNESCO World Heritage site in the cedars

The gilded, gloriously over-the-top Toshogu Shrine, resting place of the shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu, wearing snow. The famous sleeping cat, the three wise monkeys, gold leaf against dark cedar. On the way in, the vermilion arc of the Shinkyo Bridge over the icy river. There are stairs and uneven stone, so mind the footing, but this is a walking group and it is glorious.

📍 Toshogu Shrine 📍 Shinkyo Bridge
3

❄️ Kegon Falls, turned to ice

⏰ Afternoon · Up the Irohazaka switchbacks · Elevator ~9:00 AM to 4:30 PM in winter

Up the winding Irohazaka road to Kegon Falls, a near 100-metre cascade that in deep winter partly freezes into pale blue ice, one of Nikko's great sights and the whole reason to come in January. Then the still, white expanse of Lake Chuzenji and the sweeping view from Akechidaira.

📍 Kegon Falls 📍 Lake Chuzenji
How to book it and winter notes: TheTokyoTravel runs a private Nikko day tour with hotel pickup priced for groups of nine to twenty, which fits your fourteen exactly. Book the private option and tell them the group size. Dress properly warm, this is mountain cold, well below Tokyo, with snow underfoot. The Kegon elevator runs shorter winter hours (about 9:00 AM to 4:30 PM), and a private guide simply reroutes if snow closes anything higher up. Reserve by November.
🍲 Dinner: something to thaw you out

You will be back in Tokyo around 7:00 PM, cold to the bone. Two things fix that. First, Thermae-Yu, a real natural hot spring about 5 minutes from the hotel in Kabukicho, with six indoor baths, an open-air bath and saunas, open 24 hours. Soaking off a Nikko snow day in an actual onsen without leaving Shinjuku is the best-value thirty minutes of this trip, and it costs around ¥2,200 booked online. Anyone with tattoos should check the policy first, it is strict. Then hot pot at MO-MO-PARADISE Kabukicho, the original branch, open since 1993 and a few minutes from the hotel: all-you-can-eat shabu-shabu and sukiyaki with F1 wagyu, free-flow vegetables wheeled round on carts, and an all-you-can-drink add-on. Unlimited meat and unlimited beer after a snow day, with nobody counting.

Their signature is the Oni-mori, an absurd seven-fish sashimi platter, plus yakitori and hot pots. Open Sundays, 5 minutes from the station.

Wed 20 & Sat 23 · The alleys
🏮 Omoide Yokocho, then Golden Gai

Do them in this order, because they do different jobs. Omoide Yokocho is the food alley: around 80 tiny stalls wedged against the train tracks, open-fronted charcoal grills, yakitori at roughly ¥150 to 250 a skewer, beer ¥600 to 800, highballs ¥400 to 600. No reservations, no cover charge at most stalls, turnover is fast so if the stools are full just try the next one. Best between 7 and 10 PM. The one people always name is Kabuto, a no-frills stall that grills only freshwater eel, and Bar Albatross for its chandelier.

Golden Gai is the second stop, after dinner, and it is about drinking rather than eating: over 200 miniature themed bars, each seating 5 to 10, which is exactly why fourteen of you will split into clusters. For bars that reliably welcome foreigners and have English-speaking staff, the names that come up again and again are Albatross G, Bar Plastic Model and Cambiare.

Two Kabukicho warnings, because your hotel is in it. First, the outer streets around Kabukicho have an ongoing problem with touts running overcharging scams: Tokyo police logged more than 360 complaints in 2025. Never follow anyone who approaches you on the street offering a bar, a club or a girl, no matter how friendly. Stick to places you chose yourselves, like everything on this page. Second, in Golden Gai, photographing the alleys needs permission from the local business association and photographing inside a bar needs the owner's consent, so always ask "Shashin ii desu ka?" before raising a camera.
🗝️ The magic words when you book

Ask for a private room (koshitsu) and a course with nomihoudai (all-you-can-drink for a set time, usually 90 minutes to 2 hours). This fixes the price per head, keeps all fourteen of you in one room, and keeps the sake flowing without anyone counting glasses.

🛎️ Let the front desk do the calling

The hotel can phone and reserve tables for fourteen far more smoothly than you can from Bicol, especially anywhere without English online booking. Hand them your dinner list at check-in on Jan 20, or email ahead. For a big group in Tokyo this is the single best trick there is.

Shopping · What and Where

Shinjuku is one of Tokyo's great shopping hubs, with Harajuku and Shibuya a few minutes away and Asakusa for souvenirs on the last day. Bring passports for tax-free over ¥5,000 per store, and remember you each have 2 checked bags, so shop without fear.

Steps from your room: Isetan, Tokyo's most famous department store, with a food hall worth the trip alone, plus NEWoMan and Lumine over the station, the enormous Bic Camera and Yodobashi electronics stores, and Uniqlo and drugstore flagships. Perfect for shopping right up until dinner.

🧥 What to Wear · Real Winter, Colder in Nikko

The formula, every day: ① Heattech or thermal top and bottom → ② sweater or fleece → ③ an insulated winter jacket (padded or down, hooded) → ④ comfortable waterproof shoes with grip, warm socks, gloves, beanie, scarf. Tokyo sits around 3 to 10 degrees, usually crisp, dry and sunny. Nikko is far colder, often below freezing with snow, so add an extra layer for the Jan 22 mountain day. Buy kairo heat packs at the first convenience store and keep one in each pocket. Trains and malls are warm, so layers you can peel are the whole trick.

👥 Moving as a Group of Fourteen

Fourteen people move slowly, and that is fine. Set two simple habits: a group chat for the wanderers, and a fixed meeting time and spot at every regroup. On the free days, splitting into smaller clusters for an hour is perfectly good as long as dinner is the fixed point everyone returns to. The private-tour days need no such worry, the guide holds the group together.

💴 Cash · Still Matters

Cards work in the big shops, but small izakaya, market snacks and especially Golden Gai bars (cash-only, small seating charge) are cash-first. Everyone should carry ¥15,000 to 20,000. 7-Eleven ATMs reliably take foreign cards.

🚆 Getting Around · IC Cards

Your base is on top of Shinjuku Station, the world's busiest hub, so almost everywhere is a direct or one-change ride and the west-side spots are minutes away. Everyone gets a Suica or Welcome Suica IC card, or the mobile version on iPhone, for tap-and-go on every train, bus and convenience store. Note: Android phones bought abroad cannot load mobile Suica, so get the physical Welcome Suica.

🧳 Luggage · Fully Handled

Your PAL tickets include 2 checked pieces each (23 kg per piece), so big suitcases plus hand-carry are completely fine. The private coach carries everyone's bags door to door on both the arrival and departure days, so there is no train-and-suitcase juggling anywhere in this plan. On Jan 25, the hotel can hold nothing since you check out and leave straight for the airport, so pack the night before.
